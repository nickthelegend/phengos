Unit tests run on the game logic. Emulator checks run the real app in the Meta Immersive Web Emulator, with emulated hands, gaze and rooms. Nothing in the game is mocked. Desktop Chrome under the emulator is not a headset measurement.

Four ways, each one enough

  • Point. Aim the hand ray and pinch. The light floats to that palm.
  • Look. Hold your view for one second. On Quest 3 this is head-gaze, labelled "Look (head)". Quest 3 has no eye tracking. On Meta VR Glasses the same control follows your eyes.
  • One switch. An outline steps from light to light. Any pinch, from either hand, catches the outlined one. A long pinch opens the menu.
  • Voice, optional. Nine words, recognised on the device by Vosk. Nothing is stored or sent. If the model or mic is missing, the chip says so and play continues.
  • Follow the hum. Pitch is height. The pulse quickens as you aim closer. Every sound has a caption, such as "hum, left, low". Sound only hides the lights and leaves the hum.
  • High contrast. Walls and furniture redraw as yellow or white outlines. Lights become solid yellow. Geometry only: an app cannot recolour passthrough.

Your room is the level

Phengos reads the scan the headset already has: labelled furniture, plus walls, floor and ceiling classified by orientation and height. Each light is partly visible from your seat and inside a 120° turn. Depth occlusion hides it behind the real couch. A night is three rounds: 5, then 8, then 12. Caught lights stay on the ceiling. No scan within five seconds, and a paper room of three screens still finishes the game.
